I

Identity by Descent

What is the one quantity beneath relatedness and inbreeding?
video 0:42–3:51
0:51 Alike in state, alike by descent · 1:33 The kinship coefficient · 2:15 Inbreeding is kinship, one generation back · 3:07 Relatedness, rescaled

Key equations

Kinship (coancestry)
φij = P(one allele drawn from i and one from j, same locus, are IBD)
Inbreeding
Fo = φmf: an individual's inbreeding equals the kinship of its mother m and father f
Self-kinship
φii = (1 + Fi) / 2
Path counting (Wright)
φij = Σpaths (½)n (1 + FA),   n = individuals in the path through common ancestor A, counting i and j
Standard kinships (outbred)
parent–offspring ¼ · full sibs ¼ · half sibs ⅛ · avuncular ⅛ · grandparent ⅛ · first cousins 1/16
Relatedness (Wright)
rij = 2φij / √((1 + Fi)(1 + Fj));   r = 2φ when outbred
Full sibs of related parents
parents with kinship f: φFS = (1 + 2f)/4,   rFS = (1 + 2f) / (2(1 + f)) > ½

Lexicon

No. 0010:52
Identity by state (IBS)
Two alleles with the same sequence, whatever their origin. This is what genotypes show directly.
No. 0020:52
Identity by descent (IBD)
Two alleles that are copies of a single ancestral allele, inherited through the pedigree since a reference (base) generation.
No. 0031:34
Kinship coefficient (φ)
Probability that alleles drawn at random, one from each of two individuals at the same locus, are IBD. Also called coancestry.
No. 0042:02
Path counting
Wright's method: φ_ij = Σ over paths (½)^n (1 + F_A), with n individuals in each path through a common ancestor A.
No. 0052:16
Inbreeding coefficient (F)
Probability that the two alleles at a locus within one individual are IBD. Equals the kinship of the individual's parents.
No. 0062:42
Self-kinship
Kinship of an individual with itself: φ_ii = (1 + F_i) / 2.
No. 0072:54
Kinship matrix
Matrix of all pairwise kinships; off-diagonal = pairwise coancestry, diagonal = (1 + F) / 2. A genomic relationship matrix is its marker-based estimate (scaled ×2).
No. 0083:08
Relatedness (r)
Wright's coefficient of relationship: r = 2φ / √((1 + F_i)(1 + F_j)); equals 2φ for outbred individuals.

Self-check questions

  1. Compute the kinship of double first cousins by path counting. What is the inbreeding of their offspring?
  2. Explain why the kinship matrix contains inbreeding on its diagonal. Why does this dissolve the apparent chicken-and-egg problem?
  3. Why can a correctly working estimator report full-sib relatedness above 0.5?
II

The Reference Population

Identity by descent, relative to what?
video 3:51–6:37
3:58 Identical by descent, since when? · 4:46 Rare alleles carry the signal · 5:34 When structure masquerades as kinship

Key equations

Genomic relationship matrix
Aij = (1/L) Σl (xil − 2pl)(xjl − 2pl) / [2pl(1 − pl)];   x = allele dosage 0, 1, 2
What the GRM estimates
off-diagonal ≈ 2φij = rij (outbred);   diagonal ≈ 1 + Fi
Relative estimates
mean pairwise estimate in the reference sample ≈ 0, so pairs less related than average are negative
KING-robust (between-family)
φij = (NAa,Aa − 2NAA,aa) / (2NAa(i)) + ½ − ¼ (NAa(i) + NAa(j)) / NAa(i),   NAa(i) ≤ NAa(j): shared heterozygotes and opposite homozygotes, no allele frequencies
Wahlund effect
pooling groups with allele frequencies pk: heterozygote deficit = 2 Var(pk),   so apparent FIS = FST

Lexicon

No. 0093:59
Base population
The reference generation whose members are defined as unrelated and non-inbred; IBD is counted only since then.
No. 0104:25
Negative relatedness
A marker estimate below zero: the pair shares fewer alleles than the average pair in the reference sample. Expected, not an error.
No. 0114:58
Moment estimator
Kinship estimator built from allele-frequency-weighted IBS sharing (Queller–Goodnight, Lynch–Ritland, Wang, GRM). Fast; relative to the reference frequencies.
No. 0124:58
Genomic relationship matrix (GRM)
A_ij = (1/L) Σ_l (x_il − 2p_l)(x_jl − 2p_l) / 2p_l(1 − p_l); x = allele dosage (0, 1, 2). Estimates 2φ off the diagonal, 1 + F on it.
No. 0135:56
Wahlund effect
Heterozygote deficit created by pooling genetically differentiated groups; mimics inbreeding and inflates apparent kinship within groups.
No. 0146:06
KING-robust
Kinship estimator (Manichaikul et al. 2010) using only the pair's own genotypes (heterozygote sharing), robust to structure among pairs; assumes each pair is outbred and shares one ancestry.
No. 0156:20
PC-AiR / PC-Relate
Conomos et al. (2015, 2016): PCs from an unrelated subset define ancestry; kinship and F are then estimated with individual-specific allele frequencies.

Reading Relationships

What can a pair's genotypes tell you, and what can they not?
video 6:37–10:42
6:45 Cotterman's triangle · 7:43 Nine ways to share · 8:46 Segments, not loci · 9:44 Asking a relative question

Key equations

Cotterman coefficients
k0 + k1 + k2 = 1;   φ = k1/4 + k2/2
Standard (k0, k1, k2)
unrelated (1, 0, 0) · parent–offspring (0, 1, 0) · full sibs (¼, ½, ¼) · half sibs, avuncular, grandparent (½, ½, 0) · first cousins (¾, ¼, 0)
Jacquard kinship
φ = Δ1 + ½(Δ3 + Δ5 + Δ7) + ¼Δ8;   outbred: Δ7, Δ8, Δ9 = k2, k1, k0
Inbreeding from Jacquard
Fi = Δ1 + Δ2 + Δ3 + Δ4;   Fj = Δ1 + Δ2 + Δ5 + Δ6
IBD segment length
segments separated by m meioses have mean length ≈ 100/m cM; grandparent (1 meiosis) > half sibs (2) > avuncular (3)
Realized full-sib sharing
expected ½; human SD ≈ 0.04 (≈ 0.4–0.6); wider with shorter genetic maps

Lexicon

No. 0166:46
Cotterman coefficients (k0, k1, k2)
For outbred pairs, the probabilities of sharing 0, 1 or 2 alleles IBD at a locus. φ = k1/4 + k2/2.
No. 0177:20
Second-degree relatives
Half sibs, avuncular and grandparent–grandchild pairs: identical (k0, k1, k2) = (½, ½, 0), so indistinguishable with unlinked markers.
No. 0187:52
Jacquard coefficients (Δ1–Δ9)
Probabilities of the nine condensed IBD states among the four alleles of a pair, allowing inbreeding. Δ7, Δ8, Δ9 = k2, k1, k0 when outbred.
No. 0198:36
NgsRelate v2
Hanghøj et al. (2019): ML estimates of all nine Jacquard coefficients and F from genotype likelihoods; suited to low-depth data.
No. 0208:47
IBD segment
A chromosome stretch inherited intact from a common ancestor. Expected length shrinks with the number of meioses separating the pair.
No. 0219:14
Realized relatedness
The fraction of the genome actually shared IBD, which varies around the pedigree expectation because of Mendelian sampling.
No. 0229:45
Sex-biased dispersal
One sex disperses farther than the other; predicts higher kinship among nearby members of the philopatric sex.
No. 02310:17
Permutation test
Builds a null distribution by shuffling labels (here, sex) and recomputing the statistic; robust to shared baseline biases.

Measuring Inbreeding

What does heterozygosity hide, and what do runs of homozygosity reveal?
video 10:42–15:44
10:50 Inbred, yet in equilibrium · 11:48 What fakes inbreeding · 12:38 Does inbreeding vary? · 13:33 Runs of homozygosity · 14:30 Reading runs responsibly

Key equations

F_IS
FIS = 1 − HO / HE: non-random mating relative to the current population
Drift inbreeding
Ft = 1 − (1 − 1/2Ne)t;   Ht = H0(1 − 1/2Ne)t
Missing an allele
P(only one allele seen at depth d | heterozygote) = 2(½)d
g2 (identity disequilibrium)
standardized covariance in heterozygosity between pairs of loci (David et al. 2007); g2 > 0 ⇒ variance in F
F_ROH
FROH = Σ length of runs ≥ threshold / length of autosomal genome
Run length and age
common ancestor g generations back: mean run length ≈ 100 / (2g) cM; half-sib mating ⇒ F = ⅛

Lexicon

No. 02410:51
F_IS
1 − H_O / H_E: heterozygote deficit relative to Hardy–Weinberg expectation in the current sample; measures non-random mating, not total inbreeding.
No. 02511:12
Drift inbreeding
Inbreeding that accumulates in a finite population under random mating: F_t = 1 − (1 − 1/2N_e)^t. Invisible to F_IS.
No. 02611:58
Allelic dropout
Failure to observe one allele of a heterozygote (low depth, null alleles), producing false homozygotes and spurious inbreeding.
No. 02712:10
Genotype likelihood
Probability of the sequencing reads under each possible genotype; carries depth-dependent uncertainty forward instead of forcing a hard call.
No. 02812:39
Marker-based F
Individual F from genotypes: F_hom (PLINK --het), F_GRM and F_UNI (GCTA). Relative to sample allele frequencies; noisy with few loci.
No. 02913:06
Identity disequilibrium (g2)
Correlation of heterozygosity across loci caused by variance in inbreeding among individuals; g2 > 0 means F varies (Stoffel et al. 2016, inbreedR).
No. 03013:34
Autozygosity
Homozygosity because both alleles are IBD copies of one ancestral allele (as opposed to alike in state by chance).
No. 03113:46
F_ROH
Proportion of the (autosomal) genome in runs of homozygosity above a length threshold; a realized, largely frequency-free measure of inbreeding.
No. 03214:07
ROH length and age
A common ancestor g generations back gives runs of expected length ≈ 100 / 2g cM: long runs = recent inbreeding, short runs = ancient.
No. 03314:41
ROH length classes
Partitioning F_ROH by run length (e.g. < 2, 2–8, > 8 cM) to separate ancient background autozygosity from recent inbreeding.
No. 03415:07
ROH callers
bcftools roh (HMM, genotype likelihoods), RZooRoH (multi-class HMM by age), ngsF-HMM and ROHan (low coverage), PLINK --homozyg (sliding window).
